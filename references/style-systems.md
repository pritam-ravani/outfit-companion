# Style Systems

Reference for fit, color, and proportion. Read before giving fit or color advice, then
apply only the parts relevant to the user's profile — never lecture the full framework.

## Body shapes

Classify the user's silhouette by shoulder/waist/hip proportion, then dress to balance it.

| Shape | Hallmarks | Flattering strategy |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| Hourglass | Shoulders ≈ hips, defined waist | Accentuate waist; fitted or wrap silhouettes; avoid boxy cuts that hide the waist |
| Pear | Hips wider than shoulders | Add volume/interest on top; darker, cleaner lines on bottom; A-line skirts |
| Apple | Fuller midsection, slimmer limbs | Create a waistline above the natural one (empire, high-rise); V-necks elongate; avoid tight middles |
| Rectangle | Shoulders ≈ waist ≈ hips, little curve | Create curves: belted waists, peplums, layered volume; play with proportion |
| Inverted triangle | Shoulders wider than hips | Soften shoulders, add volume below (fuller skirts, wide-leg trousers); avoid shoulder pads |

**Fit notes are worth more than the label.** Two people with the same shape can carry
weight and prefer fit differently. Read their profile's "fit notes" and honor them.

## Kibbe types (image IDs)

Kibbe is a finer-grained system based on bone structure and flesh (yin/yang balance).
If the user knows their type, apply it; otherwise use body shape above and don't force it.

- **Dramatic** — long, sharp, angular; bold vertical lines, structured tailoring.
- **Soft Dramatic** — angular but fleshy; bold structure with soft draping.
- **Flamboyant Natural** — broad, blunt, natural; relaxed, oversized, unconstructed.
- **Soft Natural** — soft but broad; relaxed yet softly fitted, no sharp edges.
- **Classic** — balanced, moderate; clean, symmetrical, tailored; avoid extremes.
- **Soft Classic** — balanced with softness; smooth lines, gentle shaping.
- **Dramatic Classic** — balanced with sharpness; crisp tailoring, geometric detail.
- **Flamboyant Gamine** — compact, mixed, angular; broken lines, color blocks, sharp contrast.
- **Soft Gamine** — compact with softness; fitted, high-contrast, rounded-yet-crisp.
- **Theatrical Romantic** — petite, curvy, dramatic; sharp, ornate, very fitted.
- **Romantic** — soft, round, curvy; draped, rounded shapes, ornate detail.

## Color: 12-season analysis

Colors either harmonize with a person's natural coloring or fight it. The three axes are
**undertone** (warm vs cool), **value** (light vs deep), and **chroma** (muted vs bright).

**Determine undertone** from the profile (or ask): does silver or gold jewelry flatter
more? Are veins blue/purple (cool) or green (warm)? Does white or cream look better?

| Season | Undertone | Value | Chroma | Palette feel |
|--------|-----------|-------|--------|--------------|
| Bright Spring | Warm | Light-med | Bright | Clear, vivid warm — coral, turquoise, warm yellow |
| Warm Spring | Warm | Medium | Muted | Golden, peachy — camel, warm greens |
| Light Spring | Warm | Light | Muted | Soft pastels with warmth — peach, mint, light gold |
| Light Summer | Cool | Light | Muted | Soft light pastels — rose, lavender, powder blue |
| Cool Summer | Cool | Medium | Muted | Dusty cool — mauve, slate blue, rose brown |
| Soft Summer | Cool | Medium | Very muted | Grayed cool — dusty rose, sage, lavender-gray |
| Soft Autumn | Warm | Medium | Very muted | Grayed warm — olive, rust, warm taupe |
| Warm Autumn | Warm | Deep | Muted | Rich earth — rust, mustard, olive, warm brown |
| Deep Autumn | Warm | Deep | Muted-bright | Deep warm — burgundy, forest, dark teal, mustard |
| Deep Winter | Cool | Deep | Bright | Deep cool — black, royal blue, emerald, true red |
| Cool Winter | Cool | Deep | Bright | Icy cool — fuchsia, cobalt, crisp white |
| Bright Winter | Cool | Deep | Very bright | High contrast, clear — magenta, icy brights, black |

**How to use it:** when recommending colors or judging whether a garment suits the user,
prefer their season's palette; a piece in a wrong-season color can look draining even if
the cut is right. Don't over-apply — a loved color outside the season is fine as an accent.

## Fit & proportion rules

- A garment that fits the *widest* point (shoulders, hips) and skims the rest flatters
  more than one that's tight everywhere. Tailoring beats a smaller size.
- **The rule of thirds** — where a top and bottom meet should split the body at roughly
  1/3 : 2/3 (e.g. high-rise + tucked top lengthens legs; a mid-thigh hem on an
  untucked long top shortens them).
- Vertical lines lengthen, horizontal lines widen, and a color break at the waist cuts
  the body in two — use these deliberately.
- One focal point per outfit: choose either a statement piece or a bold color/print, and
  keep the rest quiet.

## Occasion dress codes

| Code | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| Casual | Jeans/tees/knits, comfortable, no structure required |
| Smart casual | Tailored casual — chinos/dark jeans, button-ups, knitwear, clean sneakers or loafers |
| Business casual | No jeans/sneakers (unless noted); collared shirts, blazers, chinos/dark trousers |
| Business / formal | Suit, dress shirt, leather shoes; tie optional per culture |
| Cocktail | Dark suit or sharp dress; elevated, not full-length |
| Black tie | Tuxedo or full-length formal gown |
| White tie | Most formal — tailcoat, evening dress |

Always confirm the *actual* dress code with the user — invitations and workplaces vary.
