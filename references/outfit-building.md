# Outfit Building

How to assemble outfits and wardrobes from what the user owns. Read before building
outfits or capsules.

## The outfit formula

A coherent outfit usually has one each of these roles:

1. **Base layer** — the largest visible piece (dress, or top + bottom). Sets the silhouette.
2. **Shape layer** — defines the silhouette: tucked vs untucked, high vs mid rise,
   where hems and waistlines fall.
3. **Outer layer** (as weather allows) — jacket, blazer, cardigan, overshirt. Frames the look.
4. **Color plan** — see below.
5. **Shoes + accessories** — finish and set the formality level.

When an outfit feels "off", it's usually one of: a broken proportion (rule of thirds),
a wrong color for their season, a formality mismatch, or too many competing focal points.

## Color plan (three easy schemes)

- **Neutral base + one accent** — safest. 70–80% neutrals (black/white/grey/beige/navy,
  in their undertone), one accent color from their season.
- **Tonal / monochrome** — shades of one color. Elongating and modern; vary texture so it
  doesn't flatten.
- **Two-color block** — two colors at similar intensity, separated by a neutral. Bolder;
  works when both colors suit their season.

Keep metals consistent-ish (silver vs gold) but don't be rigid about it.

## Building from the wardrobe first

1. Pick the anchor piece — the one item they most want to wear (occasion dictates it, or
   ask). Everything else supports it.
2. Match it to their season's palette and a flattering silhouette.
3. Fill the formula above using *only* items in `data/wardrobe.md`.
4. If a role can't be filled, that's a genuine wardrobe gap — name it and suggest the
   missing piece (ideally something already on their wishlist).

## Capsule wardrobes

A capsule is a small set of pieces that all mix together — typically ~20–40 items
(tops, bottoms, outerwear, shoes) sharing a palette so nearly everything matches.

Method:
1. Anchor the palette on 2–3 neutrals that suit their undertone.
2. Choose a small accent set from their color season.
3. Pick silhouettes that flatter their body shape, in 2–3 fits they like.
4. Make sure every item pairs with at least 2–3 others (the "mixability" test).
5. List which pieces they already own vs. which to add — always lead with what they have.

## Layering

- Layer from thinnest to thickest, and lightest to darkest where it reads better.
- Break up long layers at the waist to keep the rule of thirds.
- One texture contrast (smooth vs knit, matte vs sheen) adds interest without clutter.
