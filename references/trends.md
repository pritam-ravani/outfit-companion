# Finding & Judging Trends

Read before writing a trend report.

## The fashion calendar

Trends move with the show season, which runs ~6 months ahead of retail:

- **Fall/Winter shows** (Feb–Mar) → collections land in stores Aug–Sep.
- **Spring/Summer shows** (Sep–Oct) → collections land in stores Feb–Mar.
- **Pre-fall / resort** fill the gaps and drive "what to wear now" content.

When someone asks "what's trending", anchor to *what's actually available now* (or the
upcoming season if they're planning ahead), not just what walked a runway.

## Sources (search across these, don't rely on one)

- Runway reviews & recaps: Vogue Runway, The Cut, Harper's Bazaar, GQ, Esquire.
- Street style: Who What Wear, InStyle, "street style <season> <year>".
- Retail signals: "best sellers", "trending now" on the sites the user shops.
- Resale/secondhand: trends often surface early on Depop, The RealReal, Grailed.

Search from several angles and prefer recent results — fashion writing goes stale fast.

## Judging method

For each candidate trend, run this filter:

1. **Is it a movement or noise?** A real trend shows up in multiple runway collections
   *and* in retail *and* on the street. A single celebrity wearing something once is noise.
2. **Does it flatter this specific person?** Apply their color season, body shape, and
   fit notes. This is the step that makes your report useful — a trend that fights their
   coloring or silhouette gets one line, not a paragraph.
3. **Can they afford to try it?** Sort into: use-what-they-own → cheap entry point →
   investment piece. Lead with the cheap ways in.
4. **Does it fit their life?** A red-carpet trend is irrelevant to a person who dresses
   for an office and weekends.

## Report format

For each trend: **name → what it is → suits you? (why/why not) → how to try it**
(with what they own, or the cheapest sensible entry point). End with a 2–3 line "if you
try only one" summary tied to their profile.
