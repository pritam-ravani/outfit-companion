# Dressing for Region & Climate

Read when the location or weather changes what someone should wear. The key move is to
let the *region* drive fabric and layering, and let the *profile* drive cut and color.

## Start from the region, not the calendar

"Winter" means very different things in different places. Check the profile's home
region and any destination, and think in terms of actual conditions, not the season's
name. When it matters, look up the current forecast for the specific city rather than
guessing.

## Climate playbook

### Hot & humid (e.g. coastal India; the tropics)
- **Fabrics:** breathable naturals — cotton, linen, and light blends; avoid synthetics
  that trap heat (polyester clings and overheats).
- **Fit:** loose, airy cuts that don't sit tight against the skin; light colors reflect heat.
- **Layers:** minimal; a light open layer (linen overshirt, cotton shirt) for shade and
  AC-indoor chills, not for warmth.
- **Details:** short sleeves, breathable footwear, and dark/absorbent colors on sweat-prone areas.

### Hot & dry
- Breathable but with some coverage against sun; light colors, natural fabrics, plus a
  hat/scarf for sun; temperature drops at night mean carrying a light layer.

### Cold (e.g. north India in winter, hill stations)
- **Insulation over fashion:** the priority is staying warm — coat/jacket first, then
  style the layers underneath.
- **Layering order:** base (wicks, not cotton next to skin when it's really cold) →
  mid (sweater, fleece) → outer (wind/water-resistant). 
- **Extremities:** head, hands, and neck lose heat fastest — hat, gloves, scarf.
- **Cold-but-not-freezing:** a wool overcoat, a knit sweater, and layered tops get you
  further than one bulky piece.

### Rainy / monsoon
- Water-resistant outer layer, quick-dry fabrics, closed footwear that can get wet,
  and a dark/patterned lower half (hides splashes).

### Temperate / mild
- Default to light layering that can be added or shed — a light jacket or cardigan over
  a base layer covers most of the day.

## Layering by temperature (rough guide)

| Feels like | Baseline |
|-----------|----------|
| 30°C+ (86°F+) | Light single layer, breathable, minimal coverage for heat |
| 20–30°C (68–86°F) | One layer, natural fabrics, light open layer optional |
| 12–20°C (54–68°F) | Two layers — base + light jacket/sweater |
| 4–12°C (39–54°F) | Two–three layers + a real coat, scarf/gloves optional |
| -5–4°C (23–39°F) | Base + mid + insulated coat, hat/gloves/scarf |
| below -5°C | Full insulation system, cover all extremities |

## Multi-location (travel)

When the user is dressing for somewhere else, ask which place and dates if it isn't
clear, then switch to that destination's climate. A "pack for a cold trip" request should
produce a cold-weather list even if the user's home is hot — and note the contrast so
they don't under-pack for the cold leg of the trip.
